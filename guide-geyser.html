BUYING GUIDE / GEYSERS

How to choose a geyser: instant or storage, and what size?

THE SHORT ANSWER

For a kitchen sink, or one person's quick bucket bath, an instant geyser is enough. For a bathroom the family uses one after another, or for showers, choose a storage geyser. As a rule of thumb, 10 to 15 litres suits two or three people, and 15 to 25 litres suits four or five.

Instant or storage: what is the difference?

An instant geyser has a small tank of a few litres. It heats water quickly and suits short, small uses: a kitchen sink, a wash basin, or one bucket at a time.

A storage geyser has a larger insulated tank. It takes longer to heat, then keeps the water hot, so several people can bathe one after another. It is the right type for showers.

What size do I need?

Size it by how many people bathe one after another, and how. A bucket bath needs less hot water than a shower.

A common rule of thumb from manufacturers is 3 to 6 litres for one person, 10 to 15 litres for two or three people, and 15 to 25 litres for four or five. If the family uses a shower, lean towards the larger size.

The capacity in litres is printed on every geyser and its box. If you are between two sizes, the larger one saves the wait between baths on a winter morning.

What does the star rating mean?

Storage geysers carry a BEE star label, from one to five stars. The stars show how much heat the tank loses while it holds hot water. More stars means less loss, so less electricity is spent keeping the water hot.

Compare stars between geysers of the same capacity. The stars matter most if the geyser stays switched on for long periods.

What if my water is hard?

Hard water, which is common with borewells, leaves scale inside the tank and on the heating element. Scale makes a geyser slower to heat and shortens its life.

If your water is hard, ask for a model made for it: a glass-lined or anti-corrosion tank, and an anode rod that protects the tank.

Which safety features should I check?

Every geyser should have a thermostat that stops heating at the set temperature, a thermal cut-out as a backup, and a safety valve that releases excess pressure.

If you live in a tall building or use a pressure pump, tell us before you buy. The pressure a geyser is built for is printed on it, and it has to suit your water supply.

A geyser sits where water and electricity meet. Have it earthed properly and protected by an RCCB. Our MCB vs RCCB guide explains why.
