How to choose an LED bulb: watts, lumens and Kelvin

THE SHORT ANSWER

Compare lumens for brightness, not watts. Pick Kelvin for the colour of the light: warm white (around 2700K) for bedrooms and living rooms, cool white (around 6500K) for kitchens and study areas. Watts only tell you how much electricity the bulb uses.

What do watts, lumens and Kelvin mean?

Every LED bulb box carries three numbers.

Watts (W) are the electricity the bulb uses. Lumens (lm) are how much light it gives. Kelvin (K) is the colour of that light, from warm yellow to cool white.

Why should I compare lumens and not watts?

With old filament bulbs, more watts meant more light, so people learnt to buy by watts. With LEDs that habit misleads: two bulbs with the same watts can give different amounts of light.

If you want a brighter room, pick the higher lumens. If two bulbs give the same lumens, the one with fewer watts costs less to run.

Which light colour suits which room?

Warm white, around 2700K, is soft and yellowish. It suits bedrooms and living rooms, where you want to relax.

Cool white, around 6500K, is crisp and bluish. It suits kitchens, study tables and work areas, where you need to see detail.

The same room looks very different under each. If you are unsure, see both switched on side by side in the showroom before you buy.

What else should I check on the box?

Check the cap matches your holder. Most Indian homes use the B22 pin-type holder; some fittings need the E27 screw type.

Buy a known brand, check the warranty period printed on the box, and keep the bill. Warranty claims need it.
