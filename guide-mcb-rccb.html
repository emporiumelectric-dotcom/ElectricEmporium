MCB vs RCCB: do you need both?

THE SHORT ANSWER

Yes, you need both. An MCB protects your wiring and appliances from overload and short circuit. An RCCB protects people from electric shock, by cutting the power when current leaks to earth. Neither does the other's job.

What does an MCB do?

An MCB (miniature circuit breaker) trips when too much current flows through a circuit, either because too many appliances are running on it or because of a short circuit. It stops the wire from overheating and starting a fire.

An MCB does not notice a small leakage current passing through a person. That is not what it is built for.

What does an RCCB do?

An RCCB (residual current circuit breaker) compares the current going out with the current coming back. If some of it is leaking away, through a damp wall, a faulty appliance or a person, it cuts the power in a fraction of a second.

In short: the MCB protects the house from a short circuit, and the RCCB protects you.

Where does an RCCB matter most?

Wherever water and electricity are close together: bathrooms and geysers, kitchens, washing areas, outdoor points and water pumps. The risk goes up in the monsoon, when walls and switchboards get damp.

What should I ask for?

The sensitivity of an RCCB is printed on it in milliamps (mA). For shock protection at home, ask for 30 mA.

There is also a combined device, the RCBO, which does the job of an MCB and an RCCB in one unit. It is useful when the distribution board is short of space.

Anything else to know?

An RCCB is not a substitute for proper earthing. A safe home needs both.

Every RCCB has a test button. Press it from time to time, as the maker's leaflet advises, to confirm it still trips. Have the device fitted by a qualified electrician.
