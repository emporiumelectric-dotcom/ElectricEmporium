FR vs FRLS-H wire: what is the difference?

THE SHORT ANSWER

Both resist catching fire. FR-LSH wire, usually sold as FRLS-H, also gives off less smoke and less harmful halogen gas if it does burn. In a house fire, smoke harms more people than flames do, so FR-LSH is the safer choice for wiring that stays sealed inside your walls.

What do the letters mean?

FR stands for Flame Retardant. The insulation resists catching fire and does not help a flame spread along the wire.

FR-LSH stands for Flame Retardant, Low Smoke and Halogen. It does everything FR does, and when it burns it releases less smoke and less acidic halogen gas. Shops and brands often write it as FRLS-H or FRLS.

Why does smoke matter?

In a house fire, smoke usually reaches people before the flames do. Thick smoke hides the way out, and the gases in it make breathing difficult.

A wire that gives off less smoke buys time to get everyone out. That is the whole reason this grade exists.

Is it worth paying more?

House wiring is concealed. Once the walls are plastered and painted, changing the wire means breaking them open, so most homes are wired once and live with that choice for decades.

The wire is a small part of what a house costs, and the only part of the electrical work you cannot easily replace later. That is why we suggest choosing the grade first and the brand second.

What about halogen-free (HFFR) wire?

A step above FR-LSH is halogen-free wire, sold as HFFR or ZHFR. It is made for places where many people gather and getting out takes time, such as hospitals, schools and hostels. Havells sells this grade as Life Shield.

How do I check what I am buying?

The grade is printed on the insulation along the length of the wire, with the brand name, the size, the ISI mark and the standard IS 694. Read the wire itself, not only the box.

All the major brands make FR-LSH wire to the same Indian standard. So compare like with like: an FR-LSH wire from one brand against an FR-LSH wire from another, never against a plain FR wire.
